AEC segmentation: September re-run compared with August

The segmentation of the 1,211 AEC Facility applications was re-run after the scoring rubric was recalibrated. The data and the method did not change. Only the scoring rules did.

For this run, the scoring prompt was calibrated to match 29 companies hand-scored by Nathaly, covering all five partners. Latent class analysis (LCA) was then applied on the same seven factors to find the segments.

The segments held. Each comparable August segment has a close September match, at around 70% similarity.

September finds 4 segments instead of 5. One of August's five was mostly thin or incoherent applications, so it reflected how applications were written, not a type of business. It does not appear this time.

The main change is at the early stage. Pre-revenue ventures now form their own segment, and digital ventures with early revenue come together in one.

Two things drive the split: revenue stage and how physical the operations are. Collateral and market size look similar across most applicants and add little.

How many segments

The model was fitted with 2 to 15 segments. BIC measures fit while penalising extra segments, so the lowest point is the best option. September is lowest at 4 segments, with 5 almost tied. August was lowest at 5.

What changed in the scoring

  • Collateral: a past loan no longer counts as collateral, so the share of companies with some collateral fell from about half to under 10%.
  • Revenue: a repeating revenue pattern now needs a broad paying customer base shown over time. Companies at that level fell from 477 to 188.
  • Use of funds: any company at prototype stage or without a product is now "proof of concept" (45 to 251). This is what separates the pre-revenue segment.
  • Profitability: now based on revenue against all capital raised and years trading, instead of revenue per employee.

Conclusion

The segmentation is stable enough to build on. Rewriting the scoring rules changed individual scores substantially, yet the same types of business came back, organised along the same two lines: revenue stage and operational intensity. These map onto the framework's stage-related and scale-related factor blocks, which suggests the segments reflect the businesses themselves rather than the wording of the rubric.

The September result is also cleaner. The segment made of thin applications is gone, 96% of companies sit clearly in one segment, and pre-revenue ventures are separated out, which matters for matching capital to stage.

Three points to settle before the segments are used further:

  • Four or five segments. The two options fit almost equally well. The five-segment version should be reviewed to see whether the extra segment is a distinct type of business.
  • An independent accuracy check. The prompt was calibrated on the same 29 companies used to test it. A fresh set of hand-scored companies would give an accuracy figure that can be reported.
  • Collateral and market size. Both look alike across most applicants, so they add little to the segmentation as currently scored. Either the application form needs to capture them better, or they belong in the interpretation stage rather than the segmentation itself.
